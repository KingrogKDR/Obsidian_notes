## Prologue

A century-plus before the present day, Dr. Ilan Vosk — grieving his daughter's death from an undiagnosable fever — develops a compound meant to cure all disease. Self-testing it alone at night, the compound transforms him instead: his hand elongates, his nails go black, his eyes go black. Three technicians witness the aftermath; one of them, Mira Doss, later describes it as something waiting in the room finally finding a door out, using Vosk as that door. Vosk survives, changed, and lives eleven more years, watching others begin to mutate near him and then, later, at random. His final notebook entry — suppressed for decades — suggests the transformation was not an accident but a deliberate act of "generosity" from whatever came through. This event is **the Catastrophe**, Year 0 of the timeline, and the origin of the Truman mutation.

## Chapter One — Ryger's POV, age fourteen

Ryger Vay Ashvael — publicly the son of Yuna and Risc Ashvael, secretly the biological son of Yuna's late brother Timir and a Truman noblewoman of the purple-hair clan — is introduced at House Ashvael, deep in Orman territory. He bears a minor, disguisable Truman trait (a hair streak) that everyone, including Yuna, reads as an unrelated minor mutation.

Serel, the household's longtime servant, summons him to his uncle Tigor (current Lord Ashvael) with a warning to be careful "this week." In the practice yard, Ryger needles his younger cousin Adan about training form; a brief, unspoken tension surfaces around succession, since Ryger — not Adan — stands to inherit despite Adan being Tigor's trueborn son.

In Tigor's study, Ryger learns House Corvain has sent a formal marriage offer for him, addressed directly to him rather than through proper channels — a clear breach of etiquette that Tigor reads as a political test of Ashvael's willingness to bend, not a genuine courtship. Ryger feels an immediate, guilty flood of relief that the news wasn't worse — he had feared something involving the border, something that would finally demand he be useful despite his own self-perceived weakness.

Ryger seeks out Yuna in the still-room; she already knows, and gently tells him to let himself have this easier day rather than assume the worst is always coming for him.

That evening, the household holds its yearly remembrance for Timir, dead thirteen years, killed (as the household understands it) by Trumans during a minor border skirmish — the true, fuller circumstances of his death, and of Ryger's true parentage, are known only to Yuna and Risc, and even they don't know the whole truth (Yuna suspects but has never let herself confirm that the Truman woman she killed while searching for Timir's supposed kidnapper was Ryger's birth mother). Risc stands silently behind Yuna through the ceremony, as he does every year; Ryger notices, without understanding why, how careful Risc always is with his hands.

The chapter closes with Ryger going to bed thinking, guiltily, of Lae Corvain.

## Chapter Two — Three POVs: Tigor, Ambrose Corvain, Ryger

**Tigor's section.** Tigor convenes a small family council with Yuna and Risc over the Corvain letter. All three read the direct address to Ryger as a deliberate breach, not an oversight — a "hand on the table" meant to test whether Ashvael will bend. Risc lays out the trap plainly: an outright refusal costs too much against one of the Eight; answering as written cedes them the terms. Yuna proposes a third option — return the letter through the temple registrar as a quiet "clerical correction," neither accepting nor refusing, forcing Corvain to decide how much they actually want this by making them re-approach properly. Tigor agrees, though it sits uneasily with his more direct instincts. Yuna states outright that she doesn't believe the offer is really about Ryger at all — that Corvain wants something a marriage would give them access to. Tigor has Serel copy the letter for the registrar and quietly inquire whether Corvain has sent other correspondence this season. Tigor privately acknowledges Adan will feel the sting of the offer going to Ryger and not him, and decides not to tell Ryger the full political read of the situation yet, wanting him to have "this week" first — an echo of Serel's Chapter One warning.

**Corvain's section (new POV).** Introduces **Ambrose Corvain**, Lord of House Corvain, and **Coen**, his intelligence-gathering aide/advisor. Ambrose receives word of Tigor's redirect-through-the-registrar response and reads it correctly as Yuna's doing rather than Tigor's — noting it buys Ashvael a season or two without committing them to anything. He confirms internally, in front of the reader, that his interest in the match is layered: it is a message to a rival house and a genuine potential marriage both, and that he has not yet decided how much of his true aim runs _through_ Ryger versus _past_ him toward something else inside the household — deliberately left unresolved on the page. Introduces **Lae Corvain**, age fifteen, at the breakfast table; she has already read the letter herself and challenges her father directly, pointing out the offer was sent as a political message rather than a proposal to a person. Ambrose doesn't fully deny it, but tells her the two purposes aren't mutually exclusive and that he's made worse bets on worse houses. He instructs Coen to send Corvain's own reply back slower than Ashvael's, to make Ashvael wonder if they've caused offense. Establishes House Corvain's estate aesthetic (fountains, coin-and-flame sigil of Gemma) as a visual contrast to Ashvael's plainer, martial house.

**Ryger's section.** Yuna tells Ryger, in the still-room again, that the letter has been sent back through proper channels — not a refusal, a correction — meaning the matter is now open-ended rather than resolved. Ryger notices Yuna is choosing her words carefully and doesn't push at it, aware without being able to name it that there are parts of his mother's thoughts he isn't invited into. He feels relief again, and beneath the relief, a small, surprising disappointment that nothing was simply decided. He goes to find Adan in the practice yard; Adan already knows about the letter and directly confronts the succession tension ("I know I'm not the heir... I was there when I was born into it"), the sharpest and most explicit the rivalry has been stated on the page so far. Adan needles Ryger about Lae; Ryger's non-answer is itself an answer. The chapter closes with Ryger not looking toward the Godscar — nothing has started there yet — and going to bed thinking of a name with no face attached to it yet, aware, vaguely, that a season has begun without his input.
