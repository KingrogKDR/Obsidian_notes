## 1. Premise & Craft Approach

**Core Premise** A dark, gritty, realistic epic fantasy spanning multiple books, in the tonal territory of ASOIAF, Malazan, and Dune. Strong hard magic system, dense worldbuilding, political manipulation as a driving force alongside war and conflict.

**POV Structure** Single Main Character across the whole series — but told through **multiple POVs**, selected opportunistically based on narrative impact rather than a fixed rotating cast. Any character can carry a chapter if it serves the story at that moment, even a one-off. This means side characters need real motivations established in advance, even if most will never narrate.

**Planning Philosophy** Author is a natural pantser but needs structural guardrails for a multi-book series. Approach: build a **series bible of load-bearing elements only** — endpoints, hard rules, character truths, timeline — and pants everything else (scenes, dialogue, minor plot turns, side character arcs) freely within those bounds.

**Opening Structure Decision** Story opens **in medias res**, not from MC's early childhood. Specifically opens at adolescence, at the point his physical mark (see Section 3) stops being ignorable and his first major manifestation begins — see Section 4. Backstory (parents, the war, his true origin) is revealed piecemeal rather than front-loaded.

**Dramatic Irony Structure**

- The reader knows (or strongly suspects) MC is a hybrid before other characters do — dramatic irony engine, not a mystery-reveal engine.
- The reader does **not** know, at first, that MC's mother-figure is the one who killed his birth mother. This is revealed gradually as the story progresses — functions as a mid-series gut-punch reveal rather than immediate dramatic irony.

> **OPEN** — whether the eventual exposure of MC's hybrid nature threatens to unravel both societies' founding myths at a civilizational scale, or stays a contained, personal secret, is not yet decided. Worth fixing before the series reaches its endgame, since it changes the scale of the final books.

## 2. The Catastrophe & The Beings

**What Happened** Dr. Ilan Vosk, a scientist obsessed with curing all human disease, developed a compound (working name _Panacea-7_) intended as a universal cure. In private, unauthorized self-testing, the compound did not fail — it completed into something other than its intended purpose. The event, referred to in-world as **the Catastrophe**, marks Year 0 of the timeline.

**The Beings**

- Something entered the world through Vosk (or through the space he stopped occupying) at the moment of failure/completion.
- Deliberately kept mysterious to the reader — even to Vosk, the person closest to the event. No character in the prologue directly perceives or describes them.
- Vosk's final notebook line (suppressed by his estate) frames them as having agency and intent from the start: _"I did not open it for them. I opened it, and it chose, afterward, to be generous."_
- This framing implies the later Gift given to the Ormans (Section 3) is not a separate, unrelated act of mercy — it may be the same force acting again, on its own schedule, for its own reasons.

> **OPEN** — the true nature of the beings (gods, extradimensional entities, something birthed by the compound itself, etc.) is intentionally undecided in-text, but the author should privately fix an answer before drafting later books, even if it's never fully revealed to readers.

**Mutation Spread** Two waves: an initial wave of proximity-based mutation (those near Vosk during/after the event), followed by a second, later wave of mutation with no identifiable direct cause — implying the "door" no longer requires proximity to Vosk to remain open. This explains later Truman population growth without requiring individual lineage back to the original lab.

## 3. The Two Peoples

**Truman**

- Origin: intrinsic, biological mutation caused directly by the Catastrophe. Inherited. Framed culturally as evolution — earned by nature, not granted.
- Culture: science-leaning, empiricist, no gods. Frequent self-experimentation and body modification as an ongoing civilizational project.
- Highest tier: clans, organized more like scientific dynasties than bloodlines — status tied to achievement/discovery.
- Exception: the purple-hair clan (MC's mother's clan) is old-blood and out-ranks the merit clans purely by pedigree — an outlier resented from within Truman society, since it contradicts the merit-based cultural narrative. Rarely appears in public; seated deepest in Truman heartland.
- View of Ormans: godless enemies enslaved to unseen masters (the beings); mortal enemies.

**Orman**

- Origin: unmutated survivors of the Catastrophe, initially few enough that Trumans could not act on their fear/hatred of them.
- The Persecution: as Truman population grew, a political figurehead emerged among them and organized systemic, politically-driven persecution of Ormans (not simply fear-driven — it had an architect who benefited from it).
- The Gift: decades after the Catastrophe, the beings granted Ormans the ability to wield magic, explicitly in response to the persecution — shifting Ormans from victims to a people capable of resistance. Birth of Orman religion traces to this moment.
- Culture: religious, structured around purity and faith in the beings as saviors/gods. Most Ormans are unaware of the true cost their magic exacts (see Section 4) and believe in the beings superstitiously.
- Prejudice: strong, culturally normalized dislike of even mild, unrelated mutation or natural-born disability (e.g. conditions like Down syndrome, autism) — rooted in hatred of and fear of resembling Trumans. This prejudice is misapplied and does not track real Truman ancestry.
- View of Trumans: unnatural, godless, unmutated-turned-freaks; mortal enemies.

**Supporter Species** Animal species affected by the Catastrophe evolved consciousness/human-like cognition. Recruited or drawn into the war on both sides, but considered inferior by both Trumans and Ormans regardless of allegiance.

> **OPEN** — specific supporter species, their societies, and their role in current politics not yet developed. Flagged for future worldbuilding pass.

## 4. Magic System — Hard Rules

**Truman Mutation** Biological, intrinsic, inherited. Physical/structural changes to the body. Governed by science rather than faith — Trumans actively study and attempt to direct their own mutation through experimentation.

**Orman Magic — Cost** Deliberately steep and multi-layered — combines all of the following, not a single cost:

- Lifespan cost
- Sanity cost
- Physical toll
- Emotional toll
- A slow, accumulating debt to the beings — the largest and least understood cost of all

Most Ormans are unaware of the true price they pay; belief in the beings is largely superstitious rather than informed. This creates dramatic irony between what Ormans believe they're doing (worship) and what is actually happening to them (payment).

**The Hybrid — MC's Power** MC's Truman/Orman hybrid nature produces a compounded, unstable power state, combining four mechanisms into one coherent system rather than four separate rules:

- **Rejection (root cause):** Truman mutation and Orman magic are structurally incompatible in one body — like two immune systems in conflict. Early real use triggers internal conflict: seizures, blackouts, tissue damage, possible temporary regression of whichever trait isn't currently active.
- **Bleed-through (consequence):** because neither system has a clean channel, output is unreliable — reaching for one power surfaces symptoms/effects of the other. Worsens under strain; usable as a built-in tension dial in any scene involving his power.
- **Cost stacking (price):** he pays both systems' costs simultaneously rather than once, so using power costs him substantially more than a "pure" Truman or Orman. As he learns to separate the channels rather than force them through at once, this cost should mechanically decrease — his growth arc should be reflected in the magic system itself, not just narratively.
- **Detectability (existential risk):** the visible symptoms of instability read as unmistakably wrong to a trained observer — neither a clean Truman malfunction nor a clean Orman cost, which is exactly what would expose his identity to someone competent enough to recognize it.

**Arc shape:** His growth is not simply "becoming more powerful" — it is building an actual third channel where currently there is only collision. This mirrors his identity arc (constructing something new, rather than failing to be one or the other) and should be reflected mechanically as the series progresses.

**Mentorship & Growth** MC receives real guidance from a mentor, but also works significant parts out on his own through trial and consequence. Plot armor is to be kept minimal — his growth should not lean heavily on near-death-experience shortcuts.

> **OPEN** — mentor's identity, how/why they recognize or choose to help him, and how much they know about his true nature, not yet developed.

## 5. Religion & Political Structure

**Orman Religion — Inspiration & Shape** Drawing on Zoroastrian, Hindu, and Greek mythological traditions, layered rather than blended flatly:

- **Zoroastrian influence (structural/moral framework):** dualism, truth vs. lie, purity vs. corruption, fire as sacred. Underpins the purity-obsessed prejudice discussed in Section 3.
- **Hindu influence (kingship & cosmology):** divine kingship, chosen rather than strictly hereditary; candidates tested through trial/omen in a manner similar to discovery-based succession traditions.
- **Greek influence (character of the beings):** personality-driven, story-rich, occasionally contradictory rather than abstract cosmic forces — creating tension against the rigid purity framework the priesthood enforces, since the priesthood's rules may not fully reflect what the beings actually want.

**The Temple & Kingship**

- Temple hierarchy, centered on fire and purity, functions as the seat of both religious and political legitimacy.
- The King is **chosen, not born** — selected only from commoners. Nobility (Great Houses) are explicitly barred from candidacy.
- This rule was established after the Great War specifically to prevent any single Great House from gaining total regional control.
- Practical effect: Great Houses now compete for **influence over candidates** rather than the throne directly — a sublimated, ongoing political conflict rather than open succession war.

**Great Houses & Marcher Standing** Orman nobility is fractured into competing Great Houses rather than unified under one crown. House Ashvael (MC's house — see Section 7) holds hereditary border stewardship, ranking high but not highest — respected and slightly feared for the real martial strength and border intelligence it commands, and closely watched as a result.

**The Eight Great Houses**  
Orman nobility is organized around **eight Great Houses**, each dedicated to and representing one god/being of the pantheon. Every other noble family in Orman society exists as a **branch** of one of these eight — meaning the full noble hierarchy is a tree structure rooted in eight divine allegiances, not a flat list of competing families. This gives every Great House a built-in theological identity, not just a political one, and gives lesser noble houses (like potentially House Corvain, if it's a branch rather than one of the eight) a structural reason to seek influence upward rather than compete as equals.

- **House Ashvael** represents **Vay**, goddess of War and Valor. This is the being invoked in Ryger's hereditary honorific (see Section 7, MC's Name) — meaning "Vay" isn't just a name carried by Ashvael heirs, it's a direct claim of favor from the house's patron goddess specifically.
- **House Corvain** represents **Gemma**, goddess of Wealth and Fortune. Corvain is one of the Eight, not a branch house — meaning their offer for Ryger is a Great House approaching a Great House as an equal, not a lesser house angling upward. This raises the stakes of the snub in Chapter One considerably: Corvain skipping proper channels isn't a minor house testing its luck, it's one of the eight most powerful families in Orman society choosing, deliberately, not to extend Ashvael the courtesy owed between equals.

> **OPEN** — the other six Great Houses, their patron gods, and their domains are not yet named.

## 6. Geography & The Border

**Continental Divide** The continent was physically divided at the end of the Great War (see Section 8, Timeline). The border is **porous, not a hard line** — contested towns, mixed settlements, smuggling routes, and old battlefield scars, rather than a fortified wall. This supports an ongoing low-grade cold war of skirmishes, spies, and assassination rather than requiring a dramatic breach to generate conflict.

**The Border Zone — Naming Conflict** The border region is named differently by each side, reflecting cultural psychology:

- **Orman name: The Godscar** — framed as a wound the gods have not, or will not, close.
- **Truman name: The Null** — framed clinically, as a geological/scientific anomaly stripped of spiritual meaning.

**The Calamity** The Great War did not end in a clear victory. It ended in a **stalemate**, forced by an unprecedented natural calamity that neither side caused, understands, or can control. Both sides publicly claim victory and claim the other side "begged" to negotiate. The calamity has not stayed dormant since — it remains a standing, unresolved threat that could recur, and functions as an unspoken deterrent keeping both sides from resuming full-scale war without requiring either side to be reasonable or a treaty either side would actually respect.

**Midwinter**  
The final month of the year, marked by a gift-exchange custom observed between noble houses as a basic courtesy — not a religious high holiday, just an established social minimum. Corvain's total absence from this custom, year after year, is what makes their sudden direct offer read as such an obvious breach of normal etiquette.

## 7. House Ashvael & MC's Family

**House Ashvael** A Great House, religious in character (fitting the temple-and-fire framework of Orman religion), holding hereditary border stewardship. High in the noble hierarchy — not the highest, but commanding real martial strength and border intelligence others depend on.

**Family Structure**

- **MC's father**  Timir Ashvael (the direct heir who died in the war) — the direct heir to House Ashvael. An Orman noble. Secretly married/involved with MC's mother, unknown to either family.
- **MC's (birth) mother**  Zasca Reinor Vanderthal (sister of the present-day Supreme of the Vanderthal clan, Vin Ray Vanderthal) — a Truman of the purple-hair clan Vanderthal, the highest tier Truman lineage. Relationship with MC's father kept hidden from both families due to the war.
- **MC's uncle** Tigor Ashvael (Timir's brother) — now head of House Ashvael following his brother's death. Described as a good man. Has a son younger than MC.
- **MC's cousin** — the uncle's son, younger than MC. Has a legitimate blood claim to the house through his father (the current Lord), but is displaced in the succession by MC due to birth-order/age technicality — creating built-in tension the cousin may not fully understand or accept.
- **MC's mother-figure** (Yuna Ashvael (Timir's sister) — referred to in bible shorthand as "the aunt," but **never as "aunt" in-text or in MC's POV**) — unable to bear children of her own. Raises MC as her own son, publicly and totally. Neither MC nor his mother-figure know the truth: he believes she is his birth mother, full stop, and she believes he is an orphaned infant she adopted with no known connection to her — not a nephew, not the child of the woman she killed. In any scene from MC's POV, or in narration filtered through his perspective, she must be written and referred to simply as **his mother** — no hedging, no narrative tells.
- **MC's father-figure — Risc Ashvael** — Yuna's husband. Formerly a **bandit king**, a fact concealed from the outside world entirely; only House Ashvael's own members know and accept this part of his past. He had no birth surname and took the Ashvael name upon marrying into the house. Both Yuna and Risc are individually powerful in their own right — neither should read as a passive or minor presence in scenes. From MC's POV, simply his father.

> **OPEN** — how Risc, a former bandit king, came to marry into a Great House and be accepted by it (especially by Tigor, who doesn't even know the full truth about Ryger) is not yet developed. Also open: the nature and current extent of Risc's own power, and whether his past ever resurfaces as a plot thread. 

**How MC Came To Be Raised By His Mother-Figure** During the war, MC's birth parents were separated and hunted. Truman forces killed MC's father first, having heard he "kidnapped" a Truman noblewoman (MC's birth mother). When word reached House Ashvael, MC's mother-figure went searching for MC's birth mother out of a misunderstanding — and found her without the infant MC, unaware he existed. She killed her. Searching further, she found an abandoned baby — MC — with no knowledge he was connected to the woman she had just killed, and adopted him as her own son.

**The Mother-Figure's Psychology** She **suspects but suppresses** the connection — she has never let herself fully examine whether the timing and location were too convenient to be coincidence. Critically, **MC himself has no reason to suspect anything** — he was raised from infancy believing she is his birth mother, and nothing in his upbringing should signal otherwise until the story deliberately reveals it. Her suppressed unease is available as a live, simmering undercurrent in scenes involving her, long before any formal reveal, and should surface most plausibly around MC's physical mark (see below) and his first manifestation — but it must read, to MC, as ordinary maternal worry, never as something stranger.

**Why No One Suspects**  
Yuna is Timir's biological sister — meaning Ryger, who _is_ biologically Timir's son, naturally shares real Ashvael bloodline features with her: bearing, coloring, facial structure. What looks to the household like an obvious mother-son resemblance is genuine, just not through the parent-child line everyone assumes. This is why the deception has never cracked on appearance alone — there is no lie to spot, only a true resemblance traced through the wrong relationship. Critically, **Tigor (the current Lord Ashvael) does not know Ryger is Timir's son.** Only Yuna and her husband know the truth of Ryger's birth — and even they don't know the full truth (see Mother-Figure's Psychology above). Tigor believes, like everyone else, that Ryger is Yuna and her husband's biological child.

**Timir's Death Anniversary**  
This week marks the anniversary of Timir's death. It is openly and commonly mourned across House Ashvael — staff and family alike know it, including Ryger. What the household believes is only the surface truth: **Timir was killed by Trumans.** The real circumstances — the kidnapping accusation, Yuna's search, the death of Ryger's birth mother, Yuna finding and adopting the infant Ryger — remain known only to Yuna and Risc (see below). Serel's "not this week" was a plain, ordinary warning grounded in this shared, public grief — not a hint at anything hidden. The border unease is a **separate, not-yet-occurred thread** — it has not started at the time of Chapter One, and should be introduced later as its own development, layered on top of this same week only once it actually begins.

**MC's Physical Mark** A minor, disguisable Truman trait (a hair streak) has been visible since birth. Read by everyone, including his mother-figure, as a mild/unrelated mutation rather than anything Truman-linked. Draws quiet, ordinary Orman prejudice against mutation (see Section 3) — meaning MC has been discriminated against his whole life for the **wrong reason**, by people who would be far more alarmed if they understood the real one. His true Truman mutation does not manifest further until puberty / his first major use of power — the inciting incident of Book One.

**MC's Name** **Ryger Vay Ashvael.** "Vay" is the name of the god/being invoked specifically by House Ashvael's line of heirs — a hereditary honorific rather than a personal middle name, meaning it is carried only by the recognized heir, not by every Ashvael child. Every formal use of his full name is therefore a public assertion of his legitimacy as heir — useful leverage for tension in any scene where a rival house, a temple official, or his cousin's claim comes into question.

**MC's Core Personality** Locked character truth — should hold across the whole series even as plot and circumstance change around it:

- **Selfish** — not cartoonishly, but as a real, ordinary self-interest that colors his choices.
- **Weak** — both physically/magically (his powers are unreliable and carry real backlash, per Section 4) and in the ordinary human sense: he is afraid of pain and death, as any person his age would be. This fear is earned, not a flaw to be waved away — it is a direct, logical consequence of what his power actually costs him.
- **Deeply loves his family** — genuine, uncomplicated love for the people who raised him, sitting in real tension with his selfishness and fear. This contradiction (a person capable of real love who is also self-interested and afraid) is the emotional core his arc should grow out of, not away from.

**MC's Appearance**  
Ryger is striking — conventionally, notably handsome, inherited genuinely from both birth parents, who were each considered exceptionally attractive in their own societies (Timir among Orman nobility, his mother among the purple-hair clan). This is not incidental — it means people's guard tends to lower around him faster than it should, and it gives him a kind of social currency he hasn't earned through effort, which sits uneasily against his self-described weakness and dovetails with his selfishness (an easy thing to lean on rather than confront). Worth using deliberately rather than decoratively.

> **OPEN** — exactly how this personality evolves across the series (does he grow braver, more selfless, or find a harder-won version of both) is not yet decided. Worth revisiting once his arc's endpoint is fixed.

**Ryger's Age:** Fourteen, at the start of the story. This fixes Timir's death at thirteen years before "now" (matching the thirteen candles in Chapter One) — meaning Ryger was born at or very near the time of his father's death, during the same span of the war's smaller conflicts that killed both birth parents.

> **OPEN** — exact birth-to-death sequencing (was Ryger already born when Timir died, or born shortly after) not yet nailed down to the week, but doesn't need to be unless a specific scene requires it.

## 9. Prose Style Guide

This section exists to keep voice consistent across many books, many POV characters, and long gaps between writing sessions. Treat it as a hard reference, not a suggestion.

**Point of View & Tense**

- Close third-person, past tense, for all POV chapters — deep inside the character's head without switching to first person.
- **POV switches are unmarked.** No character-name headers at chapter start (not ASOIAF-style). The reader should infer whose head they're in from the first few lines — meaning the opening lines of any new POV chapter must work harder to orient the reader through voice, detail, and immediate concerns specific to that character.
- Every POV chapter — even ones dense with a character's own backstory or memory — stays in the immersive, lived-in register. A remembered event, felt by the character in the moment, must read differently than documented history (see Mythic Register below).

**Two Registers**

- _Immersive register_ (default, used for all POV chapters): present in the moment, sensory, close. Time moves at scene-pace. Even memories are experienced, not summarized.
- _Mythic/historical register_ (reserved for true prologues, interludes, and in-world documents — scholarly accounts, temple texts, legends): distanced, often narrated with more knowledge than any character had in the moment, willing to summarize years in a sentence ("He survived eleven more years..."), occasionally sliding into present tense for reflection ("He remembers the needle"). Signals that no single character's lived experience is the source — this is record, not memory. **Reserved exclusively for material outside any POV character's direct experience** — never used to color a POV chapter, even a backstory-heavy one. Even in this register, favor plain, concrete images over ornate ones — distance comes from narrative knowledge and scope, not from decorative language.

**Sentence-Level Voice**

- **Baseline is simple and direct.** Short-to-medium sentences, concrete images, plain vocabulary. The prose should be easy to visualize and understand regardless of a reader's English proficiency — clarity comes first. Avoid stacking metaphors or building sentences primarily to sound literary; a strong image stated plainly beats an ornate one stated indirectly.
- **Density is earned, not default.** Reserve longer, layered, clause-stacked sentences for select reflective or political scenes — moments of real weight (a character turning something over in their mind, a tense negotiation, a scene meant to slow the reader down on purpose). Action, danger, and ordinary scenes should stay lean and fast.
- A recurring device, used sparingly: circling a sentence back to land on an ironic or double-edged final clause. Effective in small doses in dense scenes; should not appear often enough to become a tic.
- Restrained dialogue tags. Action beats and character behavior should carry emotional weight instead of adverb-heavy tags ("he said angrily").
- Dry, understated humor in supporting characters as tonal counterweight to darker material — humor should read as incidental to character, never as the text trying to be funny.

> As new POV characters are introduced across the series, give each one a distinct texture within these rules — vocabulary, rhythm, what they notice — so voice differentiates character even without header labels.

## 10. Active Plot Threads

**House Corvain — The Marriage Offer** Corvain's true motive is a mix of two things, layered rather than singular:

- **Power politics, not really about Ryger personally** — likely tied to maneuvering against a rival Great House and/or currying favor ahead of a future king-candidate selection (see Section 5, temple succession). Ashvael's border strength makes an alliance valuable regardless of who specifically holds the heir's seat.
- **Ryger as unwitting access** — the offer is also a door into the household aimed at someone or something other than Ryger himself (candidate target not yet fixed — see OPEN note below). Ryger's relief at the offer being "just" a marriage proposal is meant to read, in hindsight, as dramatic irony — he is being used as a tool against people he loves without realizing it.

> **OPEN** — the specific target of Corvain's real interest inside the household (Adan / Lord Ashvael / border intelligence / something else) is not yet fixed. Deliberately held off for now — revisit when the scene that reveals it is being drafted.

**Staged Reveal — Suspicion of Ryger's True Nature** The first seeds of suspicion about Ryger being something other than a normal Orman are **not** planted by outside political maneuvering this early. They are explicitly deferred until **after the power-leak / coming-of-age ceremony event** (see Section 4, the duel/trial-of-status outcome of the slow-unraveling setup). Once his instability becomes visible in public — during the ceremony he wins despite it — the keenest observers present begin to privately doubt what they saw. This is where suspicion about Ryger reactivates, now grounded in something they actually witnessed rather than blind guesswork.

**Ryger & Lae — Slow-Burn Romance**  
Ryger and Lae Corvain (the daughter proposed in the marriage offer) fall genuinely in love over time — a slow burn, not immediate. Eventually, **Ryger loses Lae**, and her loss becomes one of his core motivations to grow stronger. (Cause of loss not yet decided — worth fixing once we're closer to drafting it, since it will shape how much foreshadowing it needs earlier in the story.)

> **OPEN** — how Lae is lost (death, separation, betrayal, political casualty, etc.) and the timing relative to the rest of the plot are not yet decided.

**Ryger & Adan — Brotherhood Arc**  
Ryger and Adan's relationship changes significantly over the course of the story: they begin as **rivals** (rooted in the succession tension established in Chapter One) and eventually become **true brothers** — a real, earned bond rather than one owed to blood or house obligation. Worth tracking deliberately across books so the shift feels built, not sudden.
